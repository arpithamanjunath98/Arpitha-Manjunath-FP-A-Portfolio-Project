# FP&A Operating Plan, Rolling Forecast & Scenario Modelling

## Project overview

This is an independent FP&A portfolio case study built around **Northstar Consumer Brands Ltd**, a fictional multi-division UK consumer business. The project demonstrates how an FP&A analyst can move from operating assumptions and historical performance to budgeting, rolling forecasting, variance analysis, liquidity planning and CFO-level decision support.

All company names, transactions, assumptions and financial figures are **synthetic**. The project does not represent work performed for a real company.

## Business problem

Management needs a financial planning system that can answer five practical questions:

1. What should the FY2026 operating budget look like?
2. How is the latest rolling forecast performing against budget?
3. What is driving the revenue and gross-profit variance?
4. What risks and opportunities could change the year-end outcome?
5. What does the forecast mean for cash, working capital, headcount and investment decisions?

## What I built

- Driver-based FY2026 operating budget
- Jan–Sep actual + Oct–Dec rolling forecast
- Base / Upside / Downside scenario controls
- Revenue, COGS, gross profit and EBITDA schedules
- Detailed headcount and payroll plan
- Capex and depreciation schedule
- Working-capital forecast using DSO, DPO and inventory days
- 13-week cash forecast
- Price–Volume–Mix revenue bridge
- Gross-profit driver bridge
- Probability-weighted Risks & Opportunities register
- NPV, IRR, payback and sensitivity analysis for a capital project
- Formula-driven management commentary
- CFO-style management reporting pack

## Selected findings from the Base scenario

- FY2026 forecast revenue is approximately **£68.4m**, about **£0.3m below budget**.
- The revenue variance is explained by approximately **£0.26m adverse volume**, **£0.11m favourable mix**, and **£0.15m adverse price** effects.
- Forecast gross margin remains close to budget at approximately **46%**.
- The model's detailed payroll plan results in a materially different EBITDA outlook from the original high-level budget assumption, demonstrating why driver-level planning matters.
- The cash conversion cycle improves modestly by year-end in the Base scenario.
- The 13-week liquidity forecast remains above the modelled minimum-cash policy threshold.
- The illustrative warehouse automation project produces a positive NPV and an IRR above the modelled WACC.

## Tools

**Microsoft Excel** — financial modelling, scenario analysis, variance analysis, working-capital modelling, investment appraisal, dashboards and management reporting.

This project intentionally does **not** claim Power BI, SQL, Python or generative AI implementation. Those technologies are reserved for separate portfolio projects where they are actually used.

## Portfolio integrity

This is a simulated case study. Any numerical opportunities, savings, variances or recommendations refer only to the model assumptions. They should not be presented as realised results for a real employer.
